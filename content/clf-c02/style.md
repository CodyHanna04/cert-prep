- **Level:** recognition. Match a need to a service or concept: "which service...", "which is the customer's responsibility...", "which is the most cost-effective purchasing option...". The guide's verbs are identify, recognize, describe, understand, choose. Coding, architecture design, troubleshooting, implementation and load testing are out of scope for this exam, so there is no CLI syntax, no configuration steps and no deep architecture trade-offs.
- **Formats:** single-answer questions have 4 options and 1 correct answer. Multiple-response questions have 5 or more options, 2 or more correct, and the stem says exactly how many to choose ("Choose TWO."). Roughly 85 to 90 percent single, the rest multiple-response.
- **Stems:** one problem, with a short scenario of 1 to 3 sentences when it helps.
- **Distractors:** real in-scope AWS services or concepts. Similar-sounding pairs make good distractors (Shield vs WAF, Inspector vs GuardDuty, Detective vs GuardDuty).
- **Stable facts only:** avoid prices, quotas and version numbers. Where the objective needs one (S3 storage classes, support plan tiers), cite the page in `refs`.
- **Current guide (v1.3, October 2026):** use its names exactly. That means Amazon SageMaker AI, Amazon Quick (not QuickSight), Amazon AppStream 2.0 and AWS Application Migration Service. For support plans, use Basic Support, AWS Business Support+, AWS Enterprise Support and AWS Unified Operations; never Developer, Business or Enterprise On-Ramp. Use only services on the in-scope list, distractors included, and never mention rename histories or retirement dates.
- **AI/ML (task 3.7):** recognition only. Ask which service does which task: SageMaker AI builds, trains and deploys your own models; Lex builds chatbots; Polly speaks text; Rekognition analyzes images; Comprehend, Textract, Transcribe and Translate handle text and speech; Amazon Q is an AI assistant; Amazon Bedrock builds generative AI applications on foundation models through an API. Bedrock and SageMaker AI appear as answer choices on the real exam, so tell them apart, but don't go deeper into generative-AI concepts (RAG, fine-tuning, prompt technique).
- **Exact, current terms:** every option uses AWS's own current name for the service or feature, checked against docs.aws.amazon.com or aws.amazon.com. A wrong or outdated term in an answer choice teaches the learner the wrong word.
- **Teach the distinction:** each question should leave a learner able to tell the right service from its nearest neighbor. The explanation says what the decisive clue in the stem was, in plain words, for someone with up to six months of AWS exposure.
- **Tags:** the relevant services or concepts, e.g. `["iam", "mfa"]`.
